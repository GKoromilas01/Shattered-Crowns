## New start scenario: "Survivors of a fallen village"
- Player picks a continent that has abandoned villages. Cut scene: a village is destroyed.
- A handful of survivors (3-4 people) cross desert, jungle, forest, ice or snow depending on continent. The advisor guides them to the nearest uninhabited site.
- Limited supplies must be allocated so the journey succeeds; distance sets difficulty.
- Arrival: found a new village with a small castle. Their leader becomes the future king (portrait made and assigned to the starting village).
## Characters and politics
- Spies, advisors, generals (generals earned by winning wars).
- Alliances through marriages (princes, queens, generals).
## Sea
- Sea trade and sea warfare; controlling the sea gives advantage.
## Pacing target
- ~30 minutes: economy grows, then build-up, then war begins (war is slow-start, costly to the aggressor; see docs/DESIGN_GROWTH_AND_WAR.md).
## Cut scenes at major achievements (suggested)
1. Village destroyed (opening). 2. Arrival and first hearth/castle founded. 3. First harvest / first winter survived. 4. Village becomes a town (first grown settlement). 5. First trade caravan or envoy arrives. 6. First alliance or royal wedding. 7. First ship launched / sea route opened. 8. Declaration of war / first battle. 9. Victory, a general is named. 10. Town becomes a city; final coronation.

Chapels use pale local stone, tiled roofs, simple entrances and small windows; monumental churches belong to later growth. ## Market and community-scale chapel references (owner, 22 November 2026)

Warm stone streets, simple timber market stalls, worn ochre/terracotta cloth, practical baskets/crates/sacks and restrained hanging goods. Markets begin modestly and grow in activity with the settlement; game-scale access, selection and worker readability take priority over screen clutter. A village priest among a few people suggests the chapel's community role. See [market brief](MARKET_VISUAL_BRIEF.md) and [chapel brief](CHAPEL_VISUAL_BRIEF.md). ## 2026-11-23 — Vision agreed with the owner: "best RTS of all time" principles
Owner agreed to these guiding principles (not a version plan; features still need his agreement per version):
1. One-sentence core fantasy; every feature serves it or is cut.
2. A very good first 10 minutes (build, see people work, survive a small threat).
3. Few rules, real depth: about 3 resources, 6-8 unit roles, clear counters.
4. Every match tells a story: raids, betrayals, last stands, comebacks; advisor voices (Shakespearean) and a few memorable heroes.
5. Deterministic simulation as the foundation for fair online play, replays, spectating and anti-cheat (decide early; hard to retrofit).
6. Long-term learning: ranked ladders, 1v1/2v2, map variety, replay viewer, regular data-driven balance patches.
7. Living world: day/night, seasons, weather affecting play, reactive music (one song + one graphic per version).
8. Fair business model: paid game or cosmetics only; never sell power.
Open decisions for the owner: core fantasy wording, when to start multiplayer/determinism work, business model.
