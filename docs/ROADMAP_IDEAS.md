## New start scenario: "Survivors of a fallen village"
- Player picks a continent that has abandoned villages. Cut scene: a village is destroyed.
- A handful of survivors (3-4 people) cross desert, jungle, forest, ice or snow depending on continent. The advisor guides them to the nearest uninhabited site.
- Limited supplies must be allocated so the journey succeeds; distance sets difficulty.
- Arrival: found a new village with a small castle. Their leader becomes the future king (portrait made and assigned to the starting village).
## Characters and politics
- Spies, advisors, generals (generals earned by winning wars).
- Alliances through marriages (princes, queens, generals).
## Sea
- Sea trade and sea warfare; controlling the sea gives advantage.
## Pacing target
- ~30 minutes: economy grows, then build-up, then war begins (war is slow-start, costly to the aggressor; see docs/DESIGN_GROWTH_AND_WAR.md).
## Cut scenes at major achievements (suggested)
1. Village destroyed (opening). 2. Arrival and first hearth/castle founded. 3. First harvest / first winter survived. 4. Village becomes a town (first grown settlement). 5. First trade caravan or envoy arrives. 6. First alliance or royal wedding. 7. First ship launched / sea route opened. 8. Declaration of war / first battle. 9. Victory, a general is named. 10. Town becomes a city; final coronation.

## Early shipyard reference (owner, 22 November 2026)

The owner supplied three visual references for modest early river landings: small open rowing/cargo boats, rough timber staging and sheds, nets/ropes/barrels/sacks, a natural bank and a simple path to the water. Use original designs in the existing painted style; larger harbours are later progression. See [shipyard visual brief](SHIPYARD_VISUAL_BRIEF.md). This records the visual direction; no boats/naval system or new paid asset was implemented by recording it.

## Priest and chapel reference (owner, 22 November 2026)

Humble village clergy: worn ochre/brown robes, plain rope ties and a practical, modest silhouette. Chapels use pale local stone, tiled roofs, simple entrances and small windows; monumental churches belong to later growth. Original designs in the established painted style; see [chapel visual brief](CHAPEL_VISUAL_BRIEF.md).

## Market and community-scale chapel references (owner, 22 November 2026)

Warm stone streets, simple timber market stalls, worn ochre/terracotta cloth, practical baskets/crates/sacks and restrained hanging goods. Markets begin modestly and grow in activity with the settlement; game-scale access, selection and worker readability take priority over screen clutter. A village priest among a few people suggests the chapel's community role. See [market brief](MARKET_VISUAL_BRIEF.md) and [chapel brief](CHAPEL_VISUAL_BRIEF.md). These record original-art direction, not permission to copy film scenes or buy/generate assets.

Later port stage reference: sheltered rocky inlet, short stone quay/beacon, small boats close to land and larger sailing vessels offshore. Recorded in SHIPYARD_VISUAL_BRIEF.md; not added to the v0.51 implementation scope.
