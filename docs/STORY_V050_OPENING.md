## Village

*We thought tomorrow would be much like today.*

**Before the fire**

Home was not a name on a king’s map. It was the mill turning before dawn, a neighbour keeping a place at the table, children learning which orchard paths led back to the river. We had lean winters and ordinary quarrels. We had enough. When travellers spoke of war beyond the hills, we listened—and went back to our fields.

## Attack

*Then the fires beyond the hills became our own.*

**The night the roads changed**

The riders came before the warning bells. We called them barbarians; we never learned whose war had driven them to our gates. Doors that had stood open for a lifetime were broken in a night. We could not save every roof, every keepsake, every person we loved. Those who reached the road carried what they could, and helped carry those who could not walk.

## March

*We left our homes. We did not leave one another.*

## Hill

*Beyond the last ridge, there was room to begin again.*

**A place without a name**

On the road we were strangers, asking shelter where yesterday we might have offered it. We shared blankets, counted the remaining grain and kept moving. Then the hills opened onto a valley. There was water, timber and ground that could bear a harvest. No walls promised safety. But for the first time, the youngest among us asked where we would build—not how much farther we must go.

## Market

*One day these roads might bring neighbours instead of armies.*

**The town we could make**

The homes and market are a hope, not something we have already built. Imagine doors opening to neighbours, bread cooling beside a hearth, a road worth travelling because someone welcomes you at its end. Such a place will need more than stone. It will need patient work, fair bargains and the choice to care for people when supplies run short.

## Raiders

*Their shadow is a memory, not our future.*

**The first hearth**

The riders are behind us now. We begin here in peace, with a small camp and the tools to make it a home. There is no throne waiting for you, and no finished town. Give these people shelter. Keep bread on their tables. What was lost cannot be restored in a day. What comes next can still be ours.
