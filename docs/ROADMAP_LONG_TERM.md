# Long-term vision: a living society (owner brainstorm, 2026-11-18)

Status: wishlist for roughly a year out. Nothing here is built. Do not rewrite features that already exist; extend them. Companion files: ROADMAP_IDEAS.md (nearer ideas), DESIGN_GROWTH_AND_WAR.md.

## 1. Guiding idea
A living version of the world: every person has a job, a role, stats and relationships; the realm reacts to how it is ruled. The player decides by costs and benefits, optionally using real-world statistics as the outcome model.

## 2. People
- Every citizen has a job and role, and follows it. Clicking a person shows job, role, family and stats.
- Stats per person (and for people they know): wealth, power, wisdom, knowledge, health, beauty, plus character traits (ambition, loyalty, ethics, narcissism, etc.). Stats grow through school, teachers, philosophers, war, work, experience and family.
- Heirs, children of famous people and bastards raised in foreign courts; stats of the ruler's family affect their rule. Bastards may rebel when the public feels wronged.
- Elder council promotes people with great stats to positions of power and says whether they deserve it.
- Notable people gain power through money and wars won and take a bigger part in decisions. Royal guard, knights, private guards for the rich.
- Giants and great warriors, thieves, bandits, madmen, influencers, executioners, handmaidens, eunuch armies, very loyal advisors who may betray you, double agents.
- Day and night, sleep and waking, and the cycle of seasons.

## 2b. Families and growing workers (owner idea, 2026-11-18)
- Residents are not only bought with gold. A working household raises children (about two per couple when food, housing and health allow). Children grow up over game seasons and go to school, apprenticeships, university, monastery or the army, then become useful members of society (workers, artisans, sages, soldiers, merchants).
- Workers therefore come mostly "for free" from a well-run society; gold hiring stays as an emergency or immigrant option.
- Needs a pipeline: births (housing + food + happiness), childhood (school capacity), adult placement (jobs, training buildings). Education raises stats (wisdom, knowledge) and unlocks better jobs.
- Design caution: growth must be slow enough that a small village is not instantly overrun, and a collapse (hunger, plague, war) must be able to shrink the population. Existing growth/migration rules (docs/DESIGN_GROWTH_AND_WAR.md) stay; this adds a family layer on top.
- First build step: give each household a family (spouse, children) in the People model, with ages advancing over seasons, and a school building that raises child stats.

## 3. Culture and learning
- Clergy, sages, philosophers teaching kings and queens, generals teaching soldiers, writers and poets (poems of love, war, prosperity, freedom).
- Greek and Roman verse, Greek language, ancient papyrus and Egyptian styling. Riddles that bring a country's help if solved. Proverbs on loading screens and titles.
- Schools, universities, monasteries, libraries: students and sages come from these buildings, not from the Keep.

## 4. Buildings as hubs (simplified menus)
- Many choices move onto the building: Stables give horses and cavalry; Monastery/School give students and sages; Armory gives better weapons (artisans who studied build better ones); Barracks, Shipyard, Court, Tavern etc. each own their actions.
- Spend money to evolve each building.
- Courts, judges, juries and lawyers.

## 5. Animals and creatures by continent (my suggestion, to decide)
- Aurelia: horses, knights, hunting hawks.
- Frostward: wolves, reindeer sleds, mammoth-like beasts.
- Emberreach: war elephants, camels, trained cheetahs.
- Verdance: great cats, war boars, giant lizards.
- Stormisles: sea hawks and dolphin scouts, seal hunters.
- Southveil: polar bears, sled dogs, orca-like sea guardians.
- Later fantasy options: minotaurs, dragons (opt-in world style).

## 6. Sea
- Shipyards build warships and trade ships; commercial shipping beyond caravans; whoever controls the sea has the advantage (blockades, piracy, port taxes).

## 7. Law, crime and subcultures
- Poor districts have higher crime, violence, black markets and brothels; subcultures grow there. Brothel managers earn money; spies and information flow there, and some cut scenes and decisions start there.
- Justice depends on government: juries in democracies, judges in monarchies, trial by battle or no trial under tyranny. Hostages of war, slaves, kings' children held abroad.
- Punishments and executions exist as game systems (executioner role); how graphic they are shown is a rating decision (see section 12).

## 8. Politics and war
- Advisors can ask for war when conditions demand; a great economy takes a hit from war. Cost versus benefit with real numbers.
- Royal marriages, alliances, dinners of royals, advisors and generals where decisions are made; advisors may be enemy agents.
- Savage tribes that rely on pure power, not advisors.
- Siege machines (catapults and more), creatures and beasts (section 5).
- Succession: when a king dies everyone wants the crown; rituals, crowns, crows carrying the news.
- Secrets, betrayals, blackmail, double agents, vengeance, hostages.

## 9. Story and cut scenes
- Ominous opening: soldiers find mutilated corpses with no clue; things disappear; frightened horses; a rotten child's face for one moment; epic titles and music; then silence and a cut to a calm scene introducing the castle, king, family, advisors, warriors.
- Death of the king: rituals, mourning, the race for the throne.
- Comets as omens before major events.
- Chapters after destruction (new settlement etc.).
- City-life cut scenes (monastery, school, court, tavern, market).

## 10. Game modes
- A questionnaire at the start about the world the player wants (democracy, fast game, brutal, peaceful, etc.).
- Single-player first, then multiplayer.

## 11. Suggested phasing (my proposal)
1. Person model: click a citizen, see job/role/stats; day/night and seasons.
2. Building hubs: move unit and action choices onto buildings (Stables, School, Monastery, Armory).
3. Law and crime: districts, crime rate, court building.
4. Court and council: heirs, advisors, elder council, notable people.
5. Sea: shipyards, trade fleets, naval control.
6. War depth: sieges, regional animals, spies.
7. Story pack: opening horror, king's death, comets, riddles.
8. Mature-content options and modes.

## 12. Content rating note (decision needed before building section 7-9 visuals)
Owner wants dark themes (violence, torture, executions, brothels, betrayal, sexual content in cut scenes). Notes for when we build them:
- Sexual content must never involve minors, including "underage queens" or children, in any form; handmaids and young royals are shown without sexual content.
- Stores and payment processors, and age-rating bodies, restrict explicit sexual content and extreme violence; since the game will be sold, decide the target rating first. A common approach is implied or fade-to-black scenes by default with an optional mature toggle for violence.
- Real-person likenesses and real-world statistics should use public, citable data.

## Working rule (owner decision, 2026-11-18)
Build the long-term ideas in small steps, one at a time, so each can be checked by playing before the next starts. Ideas in this file are recorded, not scheduled; nothing is built until the owner says so.
