# Shattered Crowns — owner graphics reference brief

**Recorded:** 22 November 2026  
**For:** Claude and any graphics artist/AI working on Shattered Crowns  
**Source:** the owner's supplied visual references in the development conversation  
**Status:** future visual references only; explicitly not deliverables for the next version. No new art has been generated, purchased or integrated by writing this document.

## Read this first

The owner requested **similar atmosphere and practical medieval materials, not copies**. Create original faces, garments, buildings, boats, layouts and compositions. The supplied film stills are not game assets. Do not reproduce actors, recognizable costumes, franchise insignia, screenshots or exact camera compositions.

Retain the game's established painted medieval look. These references do not request a switch to live-action photorealism. Follow [ART_DIRECTION.md](ART_DIRECTION.md), [ASSET_LOG.md](ASSET_LOG.md) and [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md). The main palette is warm limestone/parchment, timber/leather/earth browns, muted ochre/terracotta, olive vegetation and deep teal-blue water. Use natural light, restrained saturation and clear silhouettes.

Do not spend credits, purchase assets, change generator privacy settings or integrate artwork merely because this brief exists. Follow the project's current asset authorization and logging process. Defining/key imagery requires the approved paid/private production workflow. Log actual new assets and usage rights before integration.

## 1. First shipyard / river landing

**Owner intent:** a modest village-scale waterfront where people really work.

- Rough timber staging, a low jetty or simple mooring posts beside a natural riverbank.
- A small boat shed, work shelter or repair stocks, rather than a royal shipbuilding complex.
- Ropes, poles, nets, barrels, baskets, crates and sacks, used sparingly and placed for a clear purpose.
- A dirt/stone path linking the water to the village. Preserve a readable entrance and loading area.
- Weathered timber, local stone, earth and calm water. Avoid polished monumental architecture at the start.

See [SHIPYARD_VISUAL_BRIEF.md](SHIPYARD_VISUAL_BRIEF.md).

## 2. Early boats

- Small open wooden rowing boats with legible ribs, benches and oars.
- Suitable for a few passengers and light cargo; practical rather than ornate.
- Moor close to shore, drawn up to the bank or alongside low timber staging.
- Original hull silhouettes and cargo arrangements; do not reproduce the reference vessel exactly.
- Workers, passengers and visible loads should reflect actual implemented orders and capacity when that gameplay exists. Do not imply a working cargo simulation through decoration alone.

## 3. Later port / harbour

**Owner reference:** a sheltered rocky inlet, small boats close to the bank and a larger sailing ship farther offshore.

- Retain a natural coastline and clearly readable navigable water.
- A short stone quay and simple beacon suit the next stage of development.
- Small boats remain close to land; larger vessels sit where their scale and draft make sense.
- Longer jetties, cranes, larger ships and a busier harbour belong to later earned growth.
- Keep a clear visual distinction between the first village landing and the developed port.

This reference does not itself add sea trade, naval combat or new playable boats to the current version.

## 4. Village market

**Owner intent:** a lived-in working market, warm and modest at the beginning.

- Simple timber stalls beside warm local stone or plaster walls.
- Worn cloth awnings in muted ochre, terracotta and earth tones; restrained hanging displays.
- Baskets, sacks, barrels and crates; fish, produce or tools appropriate to the actual economy.
- A few readable vendors, shoppers or loading workers when supported by the simulation.
- Entrances, walking paths, selected buildings and workers must remain visible at normal game zoom.
- Start with a small group of stalls. More stalls and activity can represent later town development.

**Avoid screen clutter:** the reference's density and crowd are not a target to reproduce at village scale. The owner explicitly asked for a playable, visually calm map.

See [MARKET_VISUAL_BRIEF.md](MARKET_VISUAL_BRIEF.md).

## 5. Priests

- Humble, weathered, practical figures serving a small community.
- Plain coarse robes in warm ochre, muted brown and earth tones.
- Simple rope ties, restrained accessories and an immediately readable silhouette.
- Original faces and garments. Do not reproduce the film actor or recognizable costume details.
- A priest speaking among a few villagers fits the intended community scale when actual gameplay supports it.

## 6. Chapels

- Modest pale limestone or local stone, a muted warm tiled roof and a clear entrance.
- Small windows and restrained decoration, consistent with nearby village homes.
- Human-scale surroundings, possibly a small gathering near the entrance—not a monumental cathedral or a dense crowd.
- Later prosperity may support richer buildings and garments; the first chapel should not begin at that stage.
- Keep existing selection, footprint, placement, paths and service access intact.

See [CHAPEL_VISUAL_BRIEF.md](CHAPEL_VISUAL_BRIEF.md).

## 7. Advisor meetings / council chamber

**Owner references:** a small council gathered around a timber table in a stone room, plus a closer view of one speaker across the table.

- Create an intimate, practical meeting room appropriate to the settlement's prosperity: rough local stone and simple timber furnishings at first; richer chairs and fittings only with later growth.
- Seat a few distinct advisors around a shared table. Use original faces, varied ages, practical garments and clear silhouettes to distinguish their responsibilities.
- Show listening, discussion and restrained gestures. The meeting should feel like people considering the settlement's affairs.
- Combine cool daylight from a small window with warm candle or hearth light. Preserve the atmosphere while making faces, hands and important objects readable; do not reproduce the reference's very dark exposure.
- Use a restrained selection of papers, a simple map, cups and candles. Keep the table and composition clear rather than filling the image with ornaments or food.
- Prepare a readable group composition for a council illustration and, if requested, individual advisor portraits with consistent clothing, light and palette. Leave quiet space for interface text without baking labels into the art.
- Match the established painted style. Do not copy actors, recognizable costumes, chair designs, franchise symbols or the reference's exact staging.
- Advisor imagery belongs in the existing Advisor/City Life context, opened when wanted. This reference does not request a permanent overlay, recurring interruption or a new council simulation.

## 8. Northern regions and winter travellers — future reference only

The owner supplied a wide snowy mountain landscape with a small travelling party and pack animals, followed by closer views of travellers wearing heavy dark winter layers.

- Broad snowfields, distant rugged ridges and cool blue-grey daylight; keep natural terrain forms legible rather than covering everything with uniform white.
- Exposed dark rock faces, steep snow-covered cliffs, high passes and long blue-grey ridgelines should break up the snow. A restrained peach dawn/dusk horizon may add warmth without losing the cold atmosphere.
- Small ridge-top groups or a simple temporary camp can provide scale in future illustrations; keep equipment sparse and paths understandable.
- Sparse human figures and pack animals provide scale, with a clear route and generous visual space. This is atmosphere guidance, not authorization for a new travel or animal system.
- Original northern clothing: heavy wool, weathered leather, layered cloaks, gloves and restrained fur trim; distinct, readable silhouettes against snow. Do not copy actors, recognizable uniforms or franchise emblems.
- Keep faces and equipment readable in close portraits. Match the existing painted style rather than film photography.
- These references are for later northern-region artwork, not v0.51 deliverables. No new assets or paid generation are authorized by this note.

## 9. Eastern court / queens — future reference only

Owner caption: “oriental queens.” Treat the supplied images as guidance for the game's fictional eastern courts, not as historical evidence or a single generic real-world culture.

- Original adult queens, court members and attendants with distinct faces, hairstyles, roles and personalities; avoid copying actors or recognizable franchise costumes.
- Flowing, light layered fabrics, graceful folds, restrained jewellery and clear silhouettes. Clothing should express the character's own role and realm; do not simply reproduce the reference's revealing garments.
- Warm pale stone, patterned fabrics, carved timber, flowers and leafy courtyard views; soft daylight filtered through hanging cloth or screens.
- A restrained palette of cream, muted blue/teal, earth and warm gold, consistent with the existing painted game. Keep textile patterns readable rather than visually busy.
- Portraits should communicate thoughtful authority, composure and individual character. Leave quiet areas for interface text; keep faces readable at small sizes.
- Future art direction only: no queen/court system, portraits or paid generation added to v0.51 by this reference.

## 10. Eastern priests, warlocks and garden towns — future reference only

The owner added examples of court gatherings in enclosed gardens, ceremonial figures, a robed mystic with a staff and a masked figure.

- Town/court setting: warm stone arches and courtyard walls, shaded garden paths, clipped greenery, flowering plants, patterned vessels and a few hanging lanterns. Use gardens and architecture to frame readable groups rather than a dense crowd.
- Priests and mystical figures should have their own original ceremonial clothing, posture and restrained accessories, distinct from the humble village priest and from rulers. This is a fictional realm's visual identity, not a claim about a real culture or religion.
- If a masked figure or ceremonial staff is later commissioned, design a new silhouette, material treatment and motif. Do not reproduce the reference's distinctive full-face geometric mask, bird staff, exact robes or actor likeness.
- Differentiate individual roles through silhouette, fabric, tools and gestures while preserving the painted palette and small-scale readability.
- These are optional future illustration/portrait references. They add no magic, priesthood, court or garden-town simulation to v0.51 and authorize no asset spending.

## Shared production checks

1. The result belongs beside the existing painted world, buildings and portraits.
2. Building/boat/character silhouettes remain recognizable at normal play zoom.
3. Materials and scale distinguish a village beginning from later town/port growth.
4. Decoration does not hide workers, entrances, resources, paths, selection or construction feedback.
5. Animation and visible activity correspond to real simulation, not invented orders or cargo.
6. No film likenesses, copied compositions, recognizable franchise motifs, watermarks or brand-like text.
7. Preserve the existing simulation, camera/input, footprints, saved-state compatibility and embedded-media delivery requirements.
8. New assets have documented source, commercial usage rights, generator/plan/privacy/date where applicable, integrity checks and a completed ASSET_LOG entry.

## Current implementation boundary

v0.51 work is **From Village to Town**: an earned charter and a real mixed-cargo progression through existing production and Market controls. The owner clarified that all screenshots in this brief are for later builds, not the next version. Store this direction for future asset production. It does not authorize expanding the release into ships, warfare, religious systems, new crowds or paid asset generation without following the owner's scope and the repository rules.
